Direction v2 · revision 3

One control to connect. Everything else where you'd look for it.

Home now answers one question — what is my connection doing right now — and holds no settings at all. Every control lives in exactly one place, grouped by what it affects rather than by which engine flag it sets, and each screen opens with the plain-language choice a first-time user needs. Expert controls are one tap away, or permanently open once you flip Show advanced controls.

Where things live now

Home Status only. The orb, what it's doing, live figures, and anything you need to acknowledge. No settings, no links into settings.
Routes How it gets in. Profile, endpoint and the scanner, preferred transport, discovery depth. The only home for Endpoint.
Traffic What happens to your data. Whole-device vs proxy, DNS, IPv4/IPv6, obfuscation, validation, local port.
Settings The app itself. Theme, advanced-controls switch, identity, diagnostics and log sharing, about.

What changed in this revision

  • No control appears twice. Endpoint was on Home and in Network; it now lives only under Routes. Home still reports the endpoint in use, as read-only text.
  • Settings regrouped by effect, not by flag. "Lab" and "Network" were engineer's categories. Routes and Traffic say what the settings inside them do.
  • Two audiences, one screen. Each section leads with one plain choice; the rest sits behind Advanced. Every control carries a line saying what it does and when to change it.
  • Diagnostics can be sent to you. Verbose logging, a live stream, an explicit include-list, IP and network-name redaction on by default, and a preview of the exact report before anything leaves the phone.
  • Light, dark, or follow the system. Both themes are authored rather than inverted — the light palette uses a darker emerald so text keeps its contrast on white.
  • New type. Inter for the interface — what Proton VPN, Tailscale and Mullvad ship — and IBM Plex Mono kept strictly for data that lines up: addresses, ports, round-trips, log columns. Mono no longer sets running text, which is what made the old screens feel cramped. Compare the alternates below the phone.
  • Edges you can see. Border tokens moved well clear of the surfaces they sit on and kept the same green bias as the ground, with a one-pixel top highlight on every card. Page titles came down a step so no heading takes three lines.
  • The mark is the real one. Redrawn from the launcher globe — heavy outer ring, equator, two parallels, straight and elliptical meridians — instead of an approximation.
  • Tab bar measured, not eyeballed. All four icons sit on one 24px box with matching optical mass, the icon and its label share an exact centre line, and the selected pill is derived from the tab's own rendered box — 5.5px of air above and below the group, zero offset on the pill.
